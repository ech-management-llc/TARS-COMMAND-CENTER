The Big Three Numbers
Annual Return Rate Cap Rate aka "cap rate"
—
If you bought this property in cash (no loan), this is the percent of your money it would return per year — before factoring in taxes or any growth in the property's value. Higher is generally better. NOI / all-in cost (price + rehab + closing). Pre-finance, pre-tax annual yield.
—
Monthly Cash You'd Keep Net Cash Flow aka "cash flow"
—
After collecting rent and paying the mortgage, property taxes, insurance, repairs, vacancy budget, and management fees — this is the actual money you'd put in your pocket each month. Monthly NOI minus debt service. Rated per door against the $300 floor / $400–500 target.
—
Return on Your Cash Cash-on-Cash Return aka "CoC return"
—
This is the percent return on the actual cash you put in (down payment + rehab + closing) — separate from the loan. Higher means your cash is working harder. Annual cash flow / cash invested (down + rehab + closing). Infinity = $0 cash basis (BRRRR / creative finance).

What If Things Go Sideways?
📌 What We'd Watch
  • Cash reserve. Keep a dedicated reserve on this property — one bad month or a single major repair shouldn't sink it.
  • Rent growth. If the local market ticks up, a marginal deal becomes a healthy one. Track Reventure data for this county.
  • Don't stack marginal deals. One thin deal is fine; three drag the whole portfolio under the rules.
  • Confirm the comps. Below-market and below-replacement are two of the six gates — verify with real comps before you commit.
Cap Rate — "Capitalization Rate." If you paid cash for a property (no loan), this is the annual return percent. Pros use it to compare deals quickly. Higher = better return per dollar of price.
Cash Flow — What's left over each month after rent comes in and ALL bills go out (mortgage, taxes, insurance, repairs, vacancy budget, management). The actual money in your pocket.
CoC (Cash-on-Cash) — Return on the actual cash you put in (down payment + rehab + closing). Different from cap rate because it accounts for loans. "Infinity" means you put $0 in (creative financing).
NOI (Net Operating Income) — Annual rent collected minus annual expenses (NOT including loan payments). Used to calculate cap rate.
DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) — How many times your NOI covers your mortgage payment. ECH's covenant floor is 1.25×; the deal target is 1.8×.
LTV (Loan-to-Value) — What percent of the property's value is borrowed. ECH's covenant caps this at 65%; the deal target is ≤50%.
Per-door — Cash flow expressed per rental unit. A 4-unit property earning $1,600/mo is $400 per door. ECH targets $400-500/door, $300 floor.
BRRRR — Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. A strategy where you buy a fixer, improve it, rent it, refinance to pull cash back out, then repeat. Common path to scaling a portfolio with limited starting capital.
